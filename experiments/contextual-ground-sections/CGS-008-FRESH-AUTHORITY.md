# CGS-008 — Receiver-fresh authority for reconstructable Grounds

**Research question.** When a historical observation remains cryptographically reconstructable, what additional evidence must a *current* receiver require before admitting a new effect? Can a receiver distinguish a valid past signing identity from a currently authorized one after revocation, key changes, network replay and conflicting policy histories?

**Status — November 15, 2026.** Bounded research prototype; not a deployed authorization service. Locally executed with **Node.js 22.16.0** (8/8 passing test groups), then independently checked using **Python 3.13.5 / cryptography 46.0.4** against a freshly generated public-only Ed25519 transcript, plus **15 adversarial transcript mutations rejected**. GitHub-hosted Linux/Windows and independent-job verification is separate and not claimed successful until it finishes.

CGS-008 builds on [CGS-007's historical signed E-CPU/VGI reconstruction](CGS-007-HISTORICAL-EVIDENCE.md). It does **not** claim that the synthetic observer keys here came from the GCI-005 real mTLS experiment: GCI-005's leaf certificates are the *historical* evidence; CGS-008 explores a new, explicit *current authority* protocol.

## Three predicates that must not collapse into one

For a bounded Ground G, observation E and receiver checkpoint C, separate:

1. **Historical source authenticity:** is E correctly signed by a key named in a cryptographically valid *past* authority statement? That alone proves neither physical event time nor truth.
2. **Mathematical sufficiency:** does E, if treated as an observation, determine a requested Ground intention? The earlier CGS/GCI experiments handle this separately.
3. **Present authority:** has the receiver pinned the latest acceptable signed policy, is that policy valid at its trusted time, is the source not revoked, and does E answer a receiver-issued challenge for the exact policy/Ground/sequence?

CGS-008 implements the first and third **for the declared experimental trust model**. It does not implement physical sensor truth or general VGI proof admission.

## Protocol

A static, **out-of-band pinned bootstrap** contains two distinct Ed25519 policy-root public keys, an Ed25519 receiver public key, a Ground fingerprint and a realm. Each policy is a complete snapshot of permitted observer signing identities/capabilities. It has:

- an exact monotone version and SHA-256 hash of the previous policy body;
- explicit validity start/expiry values in **synthetic receiver-clock units**;
- an Ed25519 signature from *each* of the two distinct policy roots;
- active, role-separated observer public keys and explicitly revoked previous observers.

The receiver maintains a signed checkpoint with last accepted policy version/digest, observation sequence, last receipt digest and monotone receiver-clock value. A source can only answer a signed receiver challenge bound to that checkpoint, policy hash, Ground, fresh nonce and sequence. A receiver checks two distinct active observer signatures on one challenge, and advances its checkpoint **even if their observed parity values contradict**; a conflicting pair never becomes a result.

The bounded example has two policy epochs:

| Epoch | Signed permitted observers | Test action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Alice, Bob | Both sign parity 1; experimental agreement admitted |
| 2 | Alice, Charlie; Bob explicitly revoked | Alice signs 1, Charlie signs 0; conflict refused |

An old Alice/Bob receipt is still cryptographically inspectable as historical evidence after epoch 2, but cannot be submitted as a present epoch-2 effect. A reverted, truncated or validly signed alternative epoch-2 policy fails if it disagrees with the receiver's **already pinned** checkpoint.

### Key result and counterexample

**Freshness is not a property of a signature alone.** A valid old policy and valid old Ed25519 receipt can both be replayed by an adversary. They are refused for present use when—and only when—the receiving process has retained an appropriate newer authority/checkpoint anchor.

The suite deliberately verifies that an **older checkpoint is itself a perfectly valid signed object**. Merely checking its signature does **not** detect a rollback of the entire receiver state. The unresolved dependency is a receiver-owned durable/latest-state store with atomic commit or externally witnessed monotone checkpoint. A hash chain cannot solve total-state rollback on its own.

Likewise, two separate test root keys are **cryptographically independent keys generated by one test process**, not independent administrators or fault domains. Root collusion, receiver key compromise, dishonest agreeing observers, arbitrary caller-supplied time and global policy equivocation are not solved. This research does not implement durable anti-replay across concurrent processes or persistent host crashes.

## Exact experiments and independent verification

- [authority.js](authority.js) — Node standard-library Ed25519 signing/verifying, policy chain, revocation, receiver nonce/sequence checkpoint and historical inspection.
- [test_authority.js](test_authority.js) — 8 test groups: epoch 1 agreement, epoch 2 disagreement, historical versus current identity, single/duplicated/missing signatures, signed fork, wrong Ground, cross-challenge replay, expiration, serialized receiver checkpoint and a deliberate fake-but-valid sensor observation.
- [demo_authority.js](demo_authority.js) — produces a **public-only** signed transcript with ephemeral synthetic keys. No private keys are serialized.
- [verify_authority.py](verify_authority.py) — independently implemented Python Ed25519 auditor that consumes the public transcript; checks signatures, manifest chain, receiver checkpoint ancestry, observation constraints and two distinct outcomes without importing the JavaScript producer.
- [test_public_bundle.py](test_public_bundle.py) — 15 separate mutations of the *actual just-generated public transcript*, including forged revocation, incomplete root threshold, mismatched checkpoint, forged receiver nonce and falsified signed observation values.

Reproduce using Node.js 20+ and Python 3.11+:

    node --test experiments/contextual-ground-sections/test_authority.js
    node experiments/contextual-ground-sections/demo_authority.js > /tmp/cgs008-transcript.json
    python -m pip install "cryptography>=43,<47"
    python experiments/contextual-ground-sections/verify_authority.py /tmp/cgs008-transcript.json
    python experiments/contextual-ground-sections/test_public_bundle.py /tmp/cgs008-transcript.json

The [CI workflow](../../.github/workflows/cgs008-receiver-authority.yml) runs the Node tests on Linux and Windows (Node 20 and 22), issues a fresh transcript on one hosted job, and verifies its signatures and adversarial mutations on a **different Python-only GitHub job**. The jobs are separate processes/runners, not independent administrative authorities.

## Relationship to established security research

The concepts of **threshold signatures, metadata versions, expiration and rollback resistance** have major prior art in [The Update Framework](https://theupdateframework.github.io/specification/latest/). Append-only logs and consistency proofs have prior art in [RFC 9162](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html). CGS-008's contribution is a bounded, executable **composition for observer-Ground admission**, not a novel Ed25519 scheme, universal authorization proof, fully implemented TUF protocol or transparency service.

The new research distinction is that *remembered structural evidence*, *model interpretation*, and *present permission to cause effects* can each have a different verdict.

## Next frontier

Move receiver authority from an in-memory assumed-latest checkpoint to a durable, atomic and independently witnessed monotone state across process restart. Prove that two concurrent receiver processes cannot commit the same challenge in different forks; detect root-policy equivocation across two independent receivers; and connect a real E-CPU authenticated observation to the current signed authority policy. Preserve the old GCI-005 receipts for reconstructing history without silently giving them fresh privileges.
