## Research interpretation

A cryptographically valid signature authenticates the holder of a key, not the truth of its reported physical/mathematical observation. GCI-005 demonstrated this with a **real valid signature on an intentionally wrong bit**.

Disagreement between two authenticated observers is sufficient grounds to withhold realization under the declared unanimous policy, without guessing which observer is correct. Agreement would only provide consistency, not truth against collusion or a common faulty source. The separate phase-witness audit established truth in this finite mathematical test; that witness was unavailable to the prior blind decision.

## Explicit limits

- Real TCP/TLS traffic was confined to multiple OS processes on **one hosted runner**; not independent cloud operators or physical failure domains.
- The CA was generated inside the test job, under one administrative trust domain. There is no independent external PKI.
- Observer inputs are logical recurrence states, not sensor data.
- The phase is withheld from the phase-blind verifier's workflow inputs, but not cryptographically hidden from the provider or necessarily process-isolated from other job actors.
- Local measured RTT and MailCPU frame-size observations imply no energy or internet-wide performance gain.
- Two peers with identical false values or colluding keys could pass a consensus check. This is **not Byzantine fault-tolerant consensus, majority truth, or a physical measurement attestation**.
- GitHub main is unprotected. Workflow-scoped artifact attestations establish only declared GitHub CI provenance.
