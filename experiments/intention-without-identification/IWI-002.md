# IWI-002 — Robust navigation across uncertain transition Grounds

**Research experiment, November 15, 2026.** IWI-001 guarantees a target state with an unknown initial state, assuming the supplied transition law is correct. IWI-002 introduces a strictly harder question: **can one action-and-observation policy guarantee the target when even the transition law is unknown?**

This is not a new generic formalism for belief-space planning. It is an exact finite application of the IWI-001 verifier to **immutable model identity as part of the hidden world**. It does not accept or authenticate real Grounds; hypotheses are supplied synthetic inputs.

## Correct quantifier order

A candidate Ground specifies a complete finite model, starting-state mask, and target-state mask. All candidates must agree on the number/order of actions. One candidate is presumed to govern the world for the **entire run**. A candidate is not allowed to switch to another candidate after an action or an observation.

Form the disjoint union of the pairs (candidate_id, state). Every action preserves candidate_id while transitioning the state according to that candidate's law; visible labels can be model-specific. The observer receives no initial reading. The union of candidate initial beliefs is the starting product belief; the union of per-candidate target sets is the goal. The existing IWI-001 solver and finite independent rank/loss verifier operate unchanged over this flattened machine.

This proves guaranteed realization **for all candidate laws and all their initial states**, or emits a losing-belief trap. It does not treat model disagreement as majority voting. It cannot silently discard an inconvenient candidate.

The key logical separation is:

    for each possible law L, there exists a law-specific policy P_L
    DOES NOT IMPLY
    there exists one policy P that works for every possible law L

A safe diagnostic operation may restore the second statement, but only if its predicted observations are themselves trustworthy.

## Two compatible-looking Grounds that cannot jointly guarantee a target

Two model hypotheses, each with three states, share target 0 and start state 1. State 2 is an absorbing trap. They support actions left, right, and inspect.

- **alpha:** left takes state 1 to target 0; right takes state 1 to permanent trap 2; inspect preserves the state. All outputs are 0.
- **beta:** right takes state 1 to target 0; left takes state 1 to permanent trap 2; inspect preserves the state. All outputs are 1.

Individually alpha and beta each have a one-action guaranteed path to the goal. Jointly they admit **no fixed winning word**, since choosing left or right without knowing the law irreversibly traps one candidate. However, inspect is safe for both and returns a model-distinguishing observation. A robust policy is:

    inspect -> (observe 0) left -> target
            -> (observe 1) right -> target

The audited worst-case rank is **2**, and the contingent policy is verified against each possible origin.

Now erase beta's distinguishing output (make all its outputs 0), leaving every transition unchanged. Every candidate is still separately one-action solvable. But inspection cannot reveal which action is safe: the joint belief has rank **-1** and the verifier proves a closed losing trap. A locally valid strategy has not become a universally valid strategy merely by adding one more valid Ground.

A third case shares one common reset action and ambiguous observations: both different law identities reach the goal in one step without identifying which law governed the machine. **Identification of a law is not always necessary for guaranteed realization.**

## Implementation and verification

Files:

- uncertain_ground.js compiles candidate models into one static law-state product and reuses IWI-001 rank, blind-word, trace and witness verification.
- test_uncertain_ground.js tests correct model preservation, safe diagnosis, erased-observation refusal, unknown-law convergence, independent recursive depth oracle, tamper refusal, malformed candidates, and replay of the deterministic report.
- results/uncertain_ground.json retains the candidate Grounds, full product certificates, and realized hypothetical per-origin traces.

Node 20+ standard library only, from repository root:

    node --test experiments/intention-without-identification/test_uncertain_ground.js
    node experiments/intention-without-identification/uncertain_ground.js

The independent oracle checks the finite model as **specified**. It does not attest that the hypothesized output laws or state transitions match a real receiver, and the trace pairs are **counterfactual possible-world simulations**, not observations of multiple physical executions.

## Ambitious follow-on

The next step is not to add more synthetic law IDs by hand. Have actual E-CPU receivers issue authenticated, versioned, potentially conflicting transition observations; construct all evidence-compatible partial Ground completions; synthesize a policy that succeeds across each completion; and preserve a minimal, independently checkable refusal certificate when that cannot be proven. Unknown transition entries must remain unknown, with acquisition proposals rather than guessed edges.

In the present experiment, an adversary able to falsify the diagnostic output would invalidate the guarantee. A correct synthetic certificate is not authorization to act in a hostile environment.

Related literature: [Bertoli et al. (2006), Strong planning under partial observability](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2006.01.004). The nearby repository's [CGS contextual Grounds research (PR #234)](https://github.com/Kier73/In-Formation-Space/pull/234) studies global affine section consistency; this study instead certifies universal **action policies** over conflicting candidate transition laws. The cross-Ground evidence/authority boundary remains separate from both.
