# PNG-003 — Proof-carrying nonlocal algebraic Ground discovery

**Status:** Reproducible finite experiment with independently replayed equivalence and resolution proofs. **Date:** 21–22 November 2026 (America/Toronto). **Predecessors:** [PNG-001](README.md), [PNG-002](PNG-002.md). **Scope:** single-process, source-bound synthetic Boolean formulas; no claim to resolve P versus NP or to discover a novel general SAT technique.

## Research question

PNG-002 recognized a parity equation only when *every clause of its direct CNF encoding* was already present. Could a Ground constructor instead discover useful affine structure from a Boolean description in which **no recognizable parity block exists at all**, while preserving the exact original solution set and providing independently checkable evidence for every inference?

PNG-003 develops two sound, complementary transformations:

1. **Equivalent consensus contraction.** Eliminate split/guarded constraints without deleting any source assignments, then apply PNG-002's existing parity extractor.
2. **Proved resolution inference.** Derive previously absent clauses from distinct Boolean supports, detect complete implied parity relations among those clauses, and add the resulting equations *without removing their original parent constraints*.

The important new system property is that the **construction path itself is a proof object**. The verifier does not need to trust the constructor, its ground claims, or its output digests.

## 1. Exact semantics

All formulas are CNF over a declared set of `n` Boolean variables. A clause is a disjunction of integer-identified literals; a set of clauses is their conjunction. We make two elementary classical inferences:

**Consensus contraction (equivalence, not merely equisatisfiability).** For an arbitrary clause `C` not containing `v`:

\[
 (C\lor v)\land(C\lor\neg v)\quad\equiv\quad C.
\]

Both clauses are removed, and their shared context `C` is added. This preserves the truth value of the whole formula under **every assignment to every original variable**, including `v`. If `v` no longer occurs elsewhere, it is a genuinely free coordinate; all counts retain the factor of two contributed by it. Each rewrite is recorded with exact parent clauses, pivot, and child. Replay checks the parents were present, their non-pivot literals identical, and the child correct. A partial guarded block cannot be silently contracted.

**Resolution (entailment, without deleting parents).** From `A ∨ v` and `B ∨ ¬v`, the inferred resolvent `A ∨ B` follows. Consequently:

\[
 F\models (A\lor B),\qquad F\equiv F\land(A\lor B).
\]

The constructor saturates clauses of width at most `w` under bounded resolution, with finite attempt and addition budgets. Source clauses larger than `w` are *not included* in this tier. It discovers a parity equation `⊕_{i∈S} x_i=b` only when **all the CNF clauses forbidding assignments with the wrong parity** have proof paths back to the terminal source. Such inferred equations are added redundantly, keeping all original residual clauses. They may nonetheless reduce the nullspace dimension used for exact counting. Any proof-budget cutoff retains only sound results so far.

These are standard Boolean simplification and resolution rules, not a novel SAT method. See [Certified SAT solving with GPU accelerated inprocessing](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10703-023-00432-z), [Efficient Verified (UN)SAT Certificate Checking](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10817-019-09525-z), and [Conflict-Driven XOR-Clause Learning](https://users.ics.aalto.fi/tjunttil/publications/LaitinenJunttilaNiemela_SAT2012_proofs.pdf). The latter is particularly relevant because algebraic parity reasoning can be substantially stronger than plain clausal resolution on particular formula families.

## 2. The verifiable construction

Let `F` be the original canonical CNF, `F'` the result after `k` valid consensus steps, and `L` the conjunction of all additional parity constraints represented by the selected resolution proofs. Let `B` be the conjunction of the pre-existing direct parity blocks lifted from `F'`, and let `R` be the untouched residual clauses. The verifier establishes:

\[
 F\equiv F'\equiv B\land R\equiv B\land L\land R = F^+.
\]

The first equivalence follows by replaying every consensus step. The second is checked by independently re-expanding the **complete** parity encodings and comparing the exact clause sets. The third follows because every CNF clause of every added parity equation has an explicitly checked resolution DAG from `F'`. The independent PNG-002 verifier checks the final algebraic Ground against `F+`, reconstructs its own GF(2) basis, independently enumerates a bounded number of free coordinates to confirm counts, and checks XOR contradictions. A SAT witness is additionally tested against `F` itself.

In contrast to an unqualified hash receipt, the derivation is source-bound and **semantically replayable**. Its SHA-256 values detect accidental or adversarial mutations relative to previously pinned digests, but are not signatures and do not attest physical hosts. A malicious author who can replace the input and all hashes needs a separately trusted external anchor; local consistency alone is not provenance authenticity.

One intentional constraint: selected implied XOR equations must increase affine rank or supply a first contradiction. Other implied equations can be sound but are omitted. This avoids pretending that counting 105 derivable equations equals 105 new independent coordinates. The producer tracks candidates, selected equations, rank tests, resolution attempts, final proof steps, and time independently.

### Bounded learning is not an impossibility proof

- `resolution_width=2` is the default. Wide learned relationships may remain undiscovered; resolution on narrow clauses cannot in general efficiently certify large parity phenomena.
- `max_resolution_attempts`, `max_resolution_added`, `max_contracts`, and `max_learned` are independent explicit budgets. An exhausted proof search is **not** an UNSAT conclusion.
- The underlying PNG-002 solver still either returns an exact result within its admissible search/width budget or returns **undetermined**.
- Proofs establishing a contradiction and proofs establishing exact model counts are different: one short refutation can certify UNSAT, while exact #SAT generally needs stronger counting evidence. Independent count verification here is bounded by the admitted free dimension, except special rank-only or refutation cases.

## 3. Reproducible experiment results

Seeded deterministic fixtures with Python standard library only; recorded times are single local runs on the described CPU environment. Input-fixture generation, direct-baseline construction, and independent verification are separate from the producer's `elapsed_ms`. The explicit PNG-001 baseline is **estimated** from min-fill induced factor sizes, not materialized for large cases. The following are not all in comparable units.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Dense parity with two guards | 98 / 2,816 | 0 | **2,112** | 0 | 88 / 10 | **1,024** | Count verified |
| Mixed parity/CNF with two guards | 98 / 2,828 | 0 | **2,121** | 0 | 88 / 10 | **704** | Count verified |
| Guarded odd-charge parity network | 121 / 640 | 0 | **320** | 0 | 79 / 42 | **0** | GF(2) refutation verified |
| Non-twin implication chain | 23 / 33 | 0 | **0** | **22** | 22 / 1 | **2** | Count and 67-step resolution proof verified |
| Nonlocal contradictory implication chain | 9 / 14 | 2 unit equations | 0 | 1 | 2 / 7 | **0** | Empty-clause proof + UNSAT verified |
| Incomplete guarded encoding | 5 / 15 | 0 | 7 | 0 | 0 / 5 | **17** | Exhaustively counted |
| Budgeted ordinary random CNF | 35 / 140 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 / 35 | **undetermined** | Correct refusal |

The source 96-variable dense parity fixture in PNG-002 has rank 88 and eight free variables; inserting two unconstraining guards multiplies its original exact count by four. PNG-003's first case starts from 2,816 ordinary clauses on 98 variables, discovers no direct parity blocks in those original clauses, contracts paired guards, and recovers all 88 equations; the verifier enumerates only `2^10=1,024` free assignments of the affine Ground against the source-equivalent formula. The guarded mixed case similarly changes PNG-002's 176 solutions to 704, including the four guard assignments. This is **not** a claim that solving 98 arbitrary Boolean variables is easy.

The 121-variable odd-charge instance is a classical parity-global-obstruction construction. Its 80 vertex constraints are individually consistent, but their sum forces `0=1` because every edge appears twice and charges have odd global parity. Guard contraction reveals the local 3-XOR equations, after which standard GF(2) elimination produces a separately checked refutation. **This does not imply a short plain-resolution proof for the unsimplified Tseitin family.**

The genuinely non-twin example consists of 11 implication gadgets on 12 source coordinates and 11 auxiliaries. Every gadget contains `(x_i ∨ g_i)`, `(¬g_i ∨ ¬x_{i+1})`, `(¬x_i ∨ x_{i+1})`. None is a twin consensus pair or complete direct XOR encoding. Resolution first proves new binary clauses such as `(x_i ∨ ¬x_{i+1})`, which combine with existing clauses into exact equality. Other learned clauses constrain the auxiliaries. Width-two resolution yielded 473 candidate new clauses (19,754 attempts), 105 candidate parity equations, and **22 rank-useful independent relations**. Its source-backed proof trims to 67 resolution steps; one free coordinate remains. The separate checker replays every necessary step and finds exactly two original assignments.

The 5-variable missing-clause control makes a critical distinction: deleting one masked clause permits an extra assignment. The correct count is **17**, not the 16 of the complete guarded parity encoding. PNG-003 does not infer the unsupported parity. Bounded random 35-variable search retains status **undetermined** instead of inventing a contradiction.

## 4. Verification, negative controls, and cost

**Local verification performed:** 13 PNG-003 tests, 13 PNG-002 tests, 8 PNG-001 tests = **34 tests passed** on Python 3.13. The new PNG-003 tests include 140 seeded arbitrary-CNF cases (n=2..8) and 30 seeded guarded parity cases compared against independent brute-force truth tables; predecessor suites retain 280 and 210 additional randomized tests respectively. The tests also reject missing or altered consensus parents, missing resolution steps, modified resolvents, unsupported learned parity, forged counts, source mutation, and incomplete parity blocks. The seven campaign cases independently replay proof receipts and distinguish verified count, verified refutation, and undetermined.

Measured construction costs for the guarded dense 98-variable fixture in this run: **2,112 consensus rewrites**, **0 bounded-resolution attempts** (no eligible width-two source clauses after contraction), and **about 111 ms producer elapsed time**, versus **about 311 ms independent verification**. For the 23-variable implication network, construction performed 19,754 bounded-resolution attempts and selected 22 equations, yielding a trimmed 67-step proof; producer time was about **57 ms**, independent verification about **1.7 ms**. This is not a controlled performance benchmark across hardware, algorithms, or proof systems. Raw values are in [`PNG-003-results.json`](PNG-003-results.json).

The proof receipt is deliberately auditable: `original_clauses`, `contract_steps`, `terminal_clauses`, `base_equations`, `base_residual`, `resolution_steps`, `learned_equations`, optional `resolution_empty`, `ground`, `result`, and pinned digests. The source-to-ground path can be checked in another process with no imported constructor. This verifier reuses PNG-002's *independent* basis/count verifier only after replaying the new evidence.

## 5. Reproduce

From `experiments/pnp-ground-navigation` with Python 3.11+ and no third-party packages:

```sh
python -m unittest -v test_frontier_ground.py test_algebraic_ground.py test_derived_ground.py
python run_png003.py --write-results --write-receipts
python derived_verifier.py receipts_png003/non-twin-implication-23.json
python derived_verifier.py receipts_png003/two-guards-dense-98.json
```

`run_png003.py` writes synthetic source-bound receipts into `receipts_png003` only when requested. Generated receipts are **not** all committed: reproduce them using the pinned deterministic seed and generator code. The committed result ledger records the verified case outcomes and source/ground digest anchors; it is not itself a proof for unrun external inputs. Linux/Windows and Python 3.11/3.13 are targeted by the corresponding PR's GitHub Actions matrix.

## 6. Research consequence and next step

PNG-003 exposes a three-part cost vector:

\[
 (T_{\rm equivalence\ construction},\;T_{\rm entailed\ relation\ discovery},\;T_{\rm exact\ navigation}).
\]

A compressed observer-native state is useful only if its construction, witness reconstruction, and proof obligations remain acceptable under the actual workload. **The source state count, algebraic rank, minimum proof width, number of learned clauses, and proof-checking cost are distinct parameters.** They do not collapse to one universal measure.

A next falsifiable test is to replace fixed-width resolution with a *portfolio of evidence-producing relation constructors*: bounded resolution, source-sufficient symbolic projection, affine-polynomial elimination, and restricted proof-search oracles. Each would have to provide an equivalent or entailed relation with a small trusted checker, with strict cost and witness accounting. In particular, test a family in which the shortest representation is extremely compact but discovering it requires long proofs; compare the resolution proof size against the algebraic proof size. Such a separation would illuminate the **price of discovering a computational language**, without misrepresenting P versus NP as resolved.

**Do not equate these local results with a general P=NP algorithm.** Simple guarded fixtures are deliberately structure-rich, and the nonlocal equality network is a tractable 2-SAT family. Wide parity constraints and general CNF remain subject to standard worst-case complexity. Claiming a universal polynomial solver would require a uniform total-cost analysis and a general correctness theorem that PNG-003 does not provide.
