# PNG-002 — Constructing exact algebraic observer Grounds from Boolean descriptions

**Status:** implemented synthetic experiment and checked constructive mathematics, 21 November 2026 (America/Toronto).  
**Predecessor:** [PNG-001](README.md) — cost of explicit Boolean frontiers.  
**Question:** when a Boolean description seems difficult because its ordinary clause-dependency graph is wide, can we discover a compact **exact** observer-native computational language from the description itself, prove that the language preserves the intended problem, and navigate it with costs charged to construction as well as use?

This experiment does **not** settle P versus NP, offer a novel general-purpose SAT algorithm, or claim novelty for XOR recovery or Gaussian elimination. Both are established SAT techniques. Its value is an independently auditable, cost-accounted realization of the broader Ground-navigation hypothesis using *only CNF as input*, including a global obstruction and refusals that prevent unsound compression.

## 1. What was actually constructed

The initial input is an ordinary CNF formula `F`, containing disjunctions of signed integer literals. There are **no externally marked parity equations**. The new compiler recognizes *only complete direct CNF encodings* of XOR constraints with at most four variables (configurable to five), removes the recognized clause blocks, and records equivalent parity equations over the field GF(2). The rest of the formula remains a literal CNF residual.

Its stages are:

1. **Normalize:** remove duplicate clauses and tautologies while preserving the complete original model set. Retain an explicit empty-clause contradiction.
2. **Discover:** group canonical clauses by their variable support; find all `2^(k-1)` forbidden assignments of one parity. A group is lifted only if every required clause is present. Missing one clause means **no inference**.
3. **Check discovery:** reconstruct every proposed parity equation as its complete canonical clause encoding, combine this with the residual, and require **exact equality to the normalized input clauses**. This is stronger than mere equisatisfiability: the entire assignment set is preserved.
4. **Construct algebra:** independently from the original input's declared n bits, form a GF(2) linear system, compute a rank and an affine parameterization of all its solutions via elimination.
5. **Navigate:** use the parameterization to count pure parity solutions immediately; for mixed formulas evaluate the residual in free coordinates with exact bounded search and sound pruning; where no lifted equations exist, optionally retain PNG-001's ordinary frontier method under an explicit projected-table budget.
6. **Verify:** use a separate module with its own CNF normalization, complete-parity reconstruction, *highest-pivot* linear basis algorithm (distinct from the compiler's Gauss–Jordan), original-assignment checker, and, when affordable, independent enumeration of only the free coordinates. A contradictory XOR combination proves an UNSAT result independently.

All stages run locally in one Python process using only the standard library. SHA-256 digests identify canonical source/ground bytes, **not** authentication or trusted-host authority. The source formula and original clauses are included in optional portable receipts.

## 2. The mathematical construction

A parity equation over a support `S` and bit `b` is

\[
  \bigoplus_{i\in S} x_i=b.
\]

For each assignment `a` on `S` having the **wrong** parity, the direct CNF representation includes the clause

\[
  C_a=\bigvee_{i\in S}(x_i\neq a_i).
\]

Exactly `2^(|S|-1)` such clauses forbid all and only the assignments violating the equation. Recognizing all clauses of one of these sets and replacing them by the corresponding parity equation therefore preserves **every original assignment**, not only whether at least one solution exists. Partial blocks are never lifted. After extraction,

\[
 F(x) \;\equiv\; \bigl(Ax=b\ \text{over }\mathbb F_2\bigr)\;\land\;R(x).
\]

Let the linear system have rank `r`, and assume it is consistent. A basis for its nullspace yields a constant vector `x_*` and a matrix `N` of dimension `n × d`, with `d=n-r`, such that

\[
 \{x:Ax=b\}=\{x_*+Nz:z\in\mathbb F_2^d\}.
\]

This is a bijective parameterization, since the columns of `N` are linearly independent. Consequently,

\[
  \#F=\sum_{z\in\mathbb F_2^d} [R(x_*+Nz)],
\]

and when `R` is empty the exact model count is simply `2^d` (requiring polynomial-time elimination plus bit-integer arithmetic). A mixed residual can still require exponential exploration in `d`, which is always recorded. The compiler never infers `P = NP` from a small observed `d`.

A contradictory system of parity equations can supply a **short refutation**: indices of original extracted equations whose GF(2) sum is `0=1`. The separate checker verifies both that each extracted equation re-encodes into the source clauses and that the chosen equation subset really sums to this contradiction. A finite search budget exhaustion is returned as `undetermined`, never `unsat`.

This is directly analogous, on a precisely defined Boolean family, to the role of observer quotients in [Navigable-Compute NCR-006](https://github.com/Kier73/Navigable-Compute/blob/main/docs/NCR-006.md) and constrained global sections in [CGS-001](https://github.com/Kier73/In-Formation-Space/blob/main/experiments/contextual-ground-sections/README.md): preserve exactly the distinctions needed for the allowed operations and intentions. Here the constructed native language is **a linear algebra of solution coordinates**, and the independent checker refuses any unproved claim of source equivalence.

## 3. Reproducible results

One seed-fixed single-process run produced the following results. `Width` and `estimated projected entries` describe the ordinary PNG-001 *min-fill explicit-factor representation*, not its measured runtime on the dense cases. The dense original-frontier executions were **not** run because their predicted allocation exceeds the declared 200,000-entry budget. `GF(2) row XORs` are actual algorithmic row operations, not directly comparable to table lookups. Timing below is machine/run-specific.

| Same plus three genuine CNF clauses | 96 | 707 | 88 | 88 / 8 | **176** | 39 | 1,177,993,571,019 | Exact reduced search (11 nodes), independently checked |
| Connected cubic odd-charge parity graph | 120 | 320 | 80 | 79 / 41 | **0** | 21 | 6,391,871 | Independent `0=1` refutation |

| Seeded ordinary random 3-CNF | 16 | 64 | 0 | 0 / 16 | **28** | 11 | 7,679 | Exact, independently enumerated |
| Narrow non-XOR clause family | 25 | 23 | 0 | 0 / 25 | **16,898,609** | 2 | 95 | Standard frontier fallback; model witness verified, count not independently audited |
| Seeded general random 3-CNF | 35 | 140 | 0 | 0 / 35 | **undetermined** | 24 | 54,968,319 | Budgeted refusal, not UNSAT |

The dense system is generated by 88 independent seeded four-variable parity equations and a planted satisfying assignment. Planted generation is only an **input construction method**: the compiler receives and processes the resulting 704 ordinary clauses, not the known equations, solution, or rank. The input generator's work is not included in `solve`'s duration; it is documented and deterministically replayable. For the mixed case, three additional clauses are selected to be true for the planted assignment. They remain ordinary CNF constraints after discovery.

The parity-graph UNSAT fixture uses three edge-disjoint perfect matchings to make a connected 3-regular graph on 80 vertices (120 edge variables). Every vertex demands the XOR of its three incident edge bits equal a vertex charge. One vertex has charge 1; the others have charge 0. **Each individual vertex constraint is satisfiable.** But XORing all 80 vertex equations counts each edge twice (cancels) while the total charge remains 1, yielding `0=1`. This is a global obstruction analogous to failed global-section compatibility; the independent verifier checks the actual row combination. General instances of Tseitin-type formulas are classical, not introduced by this experiment.

## 4. What is verified and what remains unknown

**Verified within explicit contracts:**

- 13 PNG-002 tests + 8 original PNG-001 tests = **21 tests passed** under Python 3.13 in the working environment.
- The new randomized test checks **280 seeded mixed CNF/parity cases** for `n=2..9` against a separate exhaustive truth-table reference; original PNG-001 also contains 210 small-state randomized cases.
- Every recovered equation is independently re-encoded to the exact original clause set; incomplete blocks do not qualify.
- Independent highest-pivot elimination checks the claimed rank. For each 96-variable experiment, the verifier separately enumerates exactly `2^8=256` free-coordinate settings and checks the **original CNF** on each decoded source assignment, obtaining exactly 256 and 176 solutions respectively.
- The 120-variable UNSAT case carries a concrete subset-of-equations contradiction certificate, independently reconstructed and checked against source clauses.
- Deliberately tampered source, forged count, altered witness, invalid row refutation, hash mismatch, missing parity block, and depleted budgets are negative controls.
- For a nonaffine case with 25 free variables, the verifier can independently validate a returned SAT witness; it does **not** claim an independently validated exact model count under the default `2^16` verification cap, although the ordinary frontier recurrence is exact by construction and the original solver reproduces the count.

**Not established:** a general fast SAT or #SAT algorithm; polynomial-time search for arbitrary exact Grounds; optimal-width elimination; efficiently certifiable general #SAT counts or all UNSAT outcomes; recovery of nonlocal XOR consequences; arbitrary-arity XOR discovery; physical distributed execution; independent cryptographic or hardware provenance. The hard-case refusal is a feature of correct epistemic accounting, not proof of hardness for that particular instance.

**Important cost split:** `T_total = T_input_construction + T_ground_compilation + T_navigation + T_witness + T_independent_verification + T_communication/precomputation`, where some terms may share implementation stages. The measured `elapsed_ms` tracks compilation, internal equivalence check, navigation, and witness validation, but **not** independent verifier time or input generation. Ledger fields record lifted clauses, rank, row XOR operations, search nodes, literal evaluations, and explicit-frontier estimates. These different units should not be collapsed into a single claimed speedup ratio.

## 5. Reproduce locally and inspect receipts

Run from `experiments/pnp-ground-navigation/`:

```sh
python -m unittest -v test_frontier_ground.py test_algebraic_ground.py
python run_png002.py --write-results --write-receipts
python ground_verifier.py receipts/dense-96-mixed-parity-cnf.json
python ground_verifier.py receipts/tseitin-120-global-unsat.json
```

`run_png002.py` writes `PNG-002-results.json` and **optional** source-bound receipt JSON files; it independently verifies every case before recording results. No third-party Python packages or network are required. On CI the tests should be invoked from the experiment directory or through discovery, keeping the predecessor's `frontier_ground.py` importable.

The receipt contains the original CNF, a candidate Ground (equations + residual), the result and ledger, source and ground SHA-256 digests. A fresh verifier reconstructs the direct parity encoding and original model set, computes the algebra independently, and checks the supplied witness/refutation/count within its declared budget. A digest preserves byte identity when compared against a previously trusted digest; it is **not a digital signature**.

## 6. Connections to the other repositories and the next frontier

- **NCR-006 / NCR-007:** Their exact observer-native quotient and nonlinear Koopman closure describe *future-observation equivalence*. PNG-002's affine nullspace is an exact quotient of the satisfiable candidate space under the **specific constraint intention**, not a general future-behavior quotient. They are similar constructive strategies with different semantics and verification obligations.
- **VGI / ProofGround:** The recovered algebraic row is a hypothesis until equivalence checking. The `0=1` subset is an independently checkable refutation; an unsuccessful bounded search is not. A natural next integration is typed provenance and proof receipts crossing independent verifiers.
- **E-CPU / E-GPU:** A compact decoder/row basis could be transported to a receiver host and executed under existing capability governance. Cost should include source byte transfer, ground construction and receipt checking on physically distinct hosts; no such execution is claimed here.
- **In-Formation-Space CKN / CGS:** Discovery can reveal a compact composition law (parity rows), and global consistency can fail despite every local relation being realizable. The new constraint case provides a constructive testbed for navigation/holonomy ideas rather than a generalization of finite-field affine graph results.
- **VL-I / VL-64:** The input description now selects a useful internal *computational language* without receiving the language as a special API input. Remaining work is to learn or derive more expressive languages while preserving exact semantics and measuring inference cost.

### Three research hypotheses that can now be tested

1. **Algebraic interface discovery beyond literal blocks.** Can a proof-producing system derive parity consequences from *combinations* of CNF clauses, not only direct complete blocks, while preserving exact assignment semantics? Baseline against CryptoMiniSat and proof-logging solvers rather than claiming XOR recovery is new.
2. **Intention-specific complexity fingerprints.** Search for a useful jointly small pair `(algebraic residual dimension d, explicit residual width w)` across task families. A small rank deficiency helps only when residual decoding does not reintroduce exponential difficulty.
3. **Portable proof-native Grounds.** Separate source construction, ground compilation, target navigation and independent checking across real hosts. Prove that a receiver can reject stale/equivocating semantic interfaces even when byte-level integrity is intact.

A central question has sharpened: **Can a requested result generate an efficiently discoverable coordinate algebra that retains everything needed to verify the result, without merely relocating the hard work to an unaccounted compiler?** This is an experimental research question, not an assertion about all NP instances.

## 7. Prior art and mathematical context

This implementation's parity recovery is a standard pattern in SAT preprocessing, not a new algorithm. Consult:

- Armin Biere, Matti Järvisalo and Benjamin Kiesl, *Preprocessing in SAT Solving*, Handbook of Satisfiability (2021): https://cca.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/BiereJarvisaloKiesl-SAT-Handbook-2021-Preprocessing-Chapter-Manuscript.pdf . Discusses detecting XOR constraints encoded in CNF.
- M. Soos, K. Nohl, C. Castelluccia, *Extending SAT Solvers to Cryptographic Problems* (SAT 2009), and the maintained [CryptoMiniSat project](https://github.com/msoos/cryptominisat), which uses XOR recovery and Gaussian elimination in much more mature solving machinery.
- [CNF Encodings of Parity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01082), for the known exponential direct-CNF size of a parity relation without auxiliary variables.
- [Conflict-Driven XOR-Clause Learning](https://users.ics.aalto.fi/tjunttil/publications/LaitinenJunttilaNiemela_SAT2012_proofs.pdf), for established combined XOR/CNF solving approaches.
- [Extending CDCL to Disjunctions of Parity Equations](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol377-sat2026/html/LIPIcs.SAT.2026.5/LIPIcs.SAT.2026.5.html) (SAT 2026): ongoing XOR-focused proof-system research.
- [Clay Mathematics Institute — P vs NP](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/): the original general complexity question remains unsolved.

**No universal solution claimed.** The interesting new object for this family is a deliberately *verified, charged* path from an intention expressed only as Boolean clauses to a sufficient native coordinate algebra. This is a tangible next step toward the repositories' broader conception of computation as mathematical-space construction and navigation.
