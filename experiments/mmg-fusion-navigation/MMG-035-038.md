## MMG-035 — two independent external directions

Let K have independent rank-one basis V_i, i=1..r, with target slice coordinates S⊂F_2^r. Pick three independent dual relations z_j annihilating S. Their common kernel H has dimension r−3. Let q1,q2 be two distinct nonzero quotient coordinates in ambient/K; over GF(2) they are independent. Choose an anchor in each coset and partition atoms by the three parities of their K-coordinate projections against z_j. Within a single parity bin, differences from the anchor lie in H. The two anchor directions add at most two dimensions. Thus if the inside atoms in H together with the selected coset differences span S, the new rank-one supporting space has dimension≤r−1. Reconstruct in the actual pair space with both external coordinates and verify every formal tensor coefficient.

Use the sparse rank≤2 difference generator from MMG-033. Enumerate incumbent pair-atom sums of weight 2–4 for n≤4 and weight 2–3 for n=5. For each rank-two difference generate its three binary rank-one decompositions; use equal quotient coordinates to group actual atoms. Sample 48 independent triples of low-support dual relations per axis. Rank bins by intersection with the residual and available independent directions, retain at most 48 options per triple; test distinct external coset pairs whose combined direction counts could cover the residual. This is a bounded catalogue and restricted candidate screen, not complete search over two-dimensional lifts.

The first combined campaign exceeded the tool execution window and produced no results. The smaller precommitted campaign was executed separately for each seed.

- Rank47: 144 constraint triples, 297840 group checks, 0 candidate pair checks; rank remains 47.

No successful reconstruction, even for the rank24 control whose earlier one-direction lift succeeds. This is negative evidence for the randomly selected triple constraints and bounded catalogue, not a failure of the prior witness or a lower bound on tensor rank. ## MMG-036 — outside-first fitting as linear consensus

Reverse the search order. For each external coset select an anchor a_0 and independent differences D_j=a_j+a_0∈K. For two groups use independent outside quotient values. Write each D_j=Σ_i M_ij V_i; let p_i be row i of M. Choose a linear map F:F_2^k→F_2^d (d=n²), with k the total number of chosen differences, such that as many equations F(p_i)=u_i as possible hold, where u_i is the original free-leg coefficient of V_i.

Then
T=Σ_i (u_i+F(p_i))⊗V_i + Σ_j F(e_j)⊗D_j.
Expand each D_j as two rank-one atoms; collect all anchor contributions within each group. This is an exact identity for every F. A coordinate satisfying F(p_i)=u_i disappears. The new product count equals the number of remaining original terms plus the number of nonzero outside terms (before any further pair merging). Select by actual net savings.

An optimistic full-support screen groups identical nonzero patterns p_i. All indices with the same p_i must receive the same output coefficient. Therefore the maximum number of canceled original atoms is bounded by U=Σ_{p≠0} max_u #{i:p_i=p,u_i=u}. The all-active candidate can only improve if U>k+g, with g the number of coset groups. If some coefficients vanish, a smaller subset can cost less, so this screen does not exclude all sparse-support fits. Implement 32/48 seeded greedy linear-consensus fits for surviving candidate groups; prioritize the most promising 80 single groups for pairs, plus 3,000 random group pairs per axis. Cap total difference dimensions at 12. No claim of complete consensus optimization.

- Rank24: 19554 candidate group configurations, 19544 full-support screens, 464 actual fit trials; rank remains 24.
- Rank47: 24681 candidate group configurations, 24681 full-support screens, 0 actual fit trials; rank remains 47.
- Rank95: 44484 candidate group configurations, 44285 full-support screens, 9536 actual fit trials; rank remains 95.

Rank95: optimistic margins reach +1, but all sampled fits fail to realize positive net savings. The rank24 control also does not rediscover rank23 through this restricted incumbent-coordinate fit. The prior successful lift can use alternative internal rank-one bases; this is a concrete reason to extend the consensus search to alternate in-span bases and sparse support, rather than fix original coordinates.

## MMG-037 — factored gauge search for the cost of interpretation

Reuse the verified rank24 and rank23 compiler witnesses of MMG-034. For coefficient matrices U,V,W in trace convention, the simultaneous transformation
(U,V,W)→(P U Q^{-1}, Q V R^{-1}, R W P^{-1})
preserves the matrix multiplication tensor. The output-coordinate leg is transposed when converting between row-major and trace convention. All gauges are from GL(3,2), which has 168 elements. Left multiplication of any gauge by a coordinate permutation only permutes variables of adjacent linear maps. True optimal XOR-circuit cost is invariant under that relabeling; choose 28 representatives modulo the six permutation matrices. The compiler heuristic itself is not claimed to be permutation invariant, and this is not exhaustive optimization over all linear circuits.

The cost of the three linear maps factors as C_A(P,Q)+C_B(Q,R)+C_C(R,P), plus r−9 additions for transposition of the third circuit. Compute 3×28²=2,352 circuit table cells, with six seeded common-subexpression syntheses per cell, 14,112 local syntheses per rank. Then evaluate all 28³=21,952 representative gauge triples by 65,856 table lookups. This minimizes the sum of the sampled local circuit costs exactly over the representative triples, without compiling a full decomposition for every triple. All selected circuits independently tested on basis inputs; full tensor verified on all 81 input basis pairs. Compile tensor powers through side81 and compare every output coordinate against classical multiplication.

## MMG-038 — incorporate established exact rank lower bounds

The small square matrices are controls for search mechanics, not viable finite-size exact-rank exponent-breaking targets. The Alder–Strassen bound for a simple matrix algebra is 2n²−1. Bläser's arbitrary-field bound is ceil(5n²/2−3n). Sources: Bläser, Lower bounds for the bilinear complexity of associative algebras, https://doi.org/10.1007/PL00001605 ; Bläser, A 5/2 n²-Lower Bound for the Rank of n×n Matrix Multiplication over Arbitrary Fields, https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/Blaser99.html . Define L(n)=max of these. For an exact fixed square rank witness, the strict exponent threshold is r^4<n^9, equivalently r≤floorFourthRoot(n^9−1). Compute all thresholds in BigInt. The established rank floor excludes the strict target for every n=2..33. At n=34, L=2788 and target=2791, the first square size not excluded by these two bounds; other bounds may exclude it, and this is not an existence statement. These are exact-rank bounds and not general border-rank bounds. They do not disprove an asymptotic exponent below 9/4.

For rectangular T(a,b,c), cyclic tensor composition yields a square tensor of side abc with rank≤r³. Hence a necessary bound is ceilCubeRoot(L(abc)); also restrict to square side min(a,b,c) to obtain L(min), and retain the previous slice bound max(ab+c−1,ac+b−1,bc+a−1). The strict target is floorFourthRoot((abc)^3−1). Screen all 19,599 sorted shapes with 1≤a≤b≤c≤48 excluding (1,1,1). Slice bound alone excluded 8,358; combined bound excludes 8,482, 124 additional shapes. First remaining by volume is (3,4,4), with lower18 and target18. This only means not excluded by the implemented bounds, not that rank18 exists or that stronger published bounds allow it. Next search should incorporate stronger rectangular lower bounds before investing in any shape.

## LGFC-001 v0.5 (proposed) — outside-first selection

Let the search state contain the target slice space, incumbent support, a library of rank-one directions and a family of latent candidate constraints. Delay choosing the constraints (which incumbent coordinates should be removed) until external structure has been discovered. The outside-first formulation yields a measurable optimistic cancellation screen followed by exact realization. Results are negative on the tested fixed-coordinate seeds; the conditional efficiency conjecture still requires matched-work tests. The gauge table experiment provides separate evidence that factoring the interpreter's own search reduces repeated compilation work while preserving admitted mathematical identities. Next: jointly navigate alternative internal bases, sparse group support and multi-coset cancellation cycles; scale to viable rectangular/decomposition-family targets rather than the excluded small square exponent gates.
