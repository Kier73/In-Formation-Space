# MMG-009–011 — Cost-constrained rectangular and linear-circuit synthesis

## Intention and admission

Let T(a,b,c) describe unrestricted multiplication of a×b by b×c matrices. Input entries are independent. A fixed exact rank-r witness yields a square witness of side abc and rank r³ by tensoring the three cyclic versions of T and permuting digit coordinates. Thus r⁴ < (abc)³ suffices to beat the square exponent 9/4. This is an exact integer admission predicate, not a floating-point comparison. More generally, a family of arbitrary n×n^α by n^α×n products with exponent 2 gives square exponent at most 6/(2+α) by cyclic tensor composition (using the rank characterization of the exponent, or formal bilinearization and tensor powers). For this route alone α>2/3 is sufficient, while α=1/2 gives 2.4. The Nov 24 manuscript claims the rectangular dual exponent is exactly 1/2, so improving it would independently challenge the manuscript. The claimed equality is the research target, not an assumption in the witness verifier.

A slice space of dimension d and minimum nonzero matrix rank m requires tensor rank at least d+m-1: kill d-1 independent coefficient forms, leaving a nonzero slice of rank ≥m and at most r-d+1 rank-one summands. For each leg of T(a,b,c), the slice matrices are, up to permutation, X tensor I_m, so the lower bound is max(ab+c-1,bc+a-1,ac+b-1). Apply this before search. Of 2,599 sorted shapes with dimensions ≤24 (excluding (1,1,1)), 1,345 cannot satisfy the strict cyclic rank target by this bound alone. The other 1,254 are not excluded by this test, not certified feasible.

Additional structural necessary condition: reshape the three factors of each rank term as matrices U_t (a×b), V_t (b×c), W_t (c×a, trace convention). Regroup the six indices with one index of each factor on either side of a cut crossing all three equality wires. The matrix multiplication tensor becomes a permutation matrix of rank abc. Each term becomes a Kronecker product, up to permutations/transposes, of rank rank(U_t) rank(V_t) rank(W_t). Hence sum_t rank(U_t) rank(V_t) rank(W_t) ≥ abc. Purely rank-one coefficient matrices on all three legs cannot reduce the abc product count. Also, each output-coordinate slice has rank b, so sum_t nnz(W_t) ≥ abc; similarly for U,V. Any subcubic *arithmetic* realization therefore needs shared translation computations or dense structured transforms instead of separately expanding every linear form. These are necessary constraints, not sufficient constructions.

## MMG-009: quotient elimination

For any axis, view the other two factors as vectors v_i in their tensor product. If v_j=sum_i λ_i v_i, redistribute the first factor a_j to the existing a_i via a_i←a_i+λ_i a_j, then remove term j. A whole family of relations is recovered by Gaussian elimination over GF(2), rather than searching only for identical pairs. Every quotient preserves the complete tensor. 500 random exact tensor comparisons plus a deliberate nonduplicate dependence. The 14 search runs took 252,000 proposed steps total (seven shapes, two seeds per shape). Quotient calls removed transient terms but produced no improved best rank in these runs. Control walk improved a tiled 4×4 seed from 56 to 55, weaker than the existing 49-product Strassen tensor power. These runs do not improve the known upper bounds. They are diagnostic tests; different RNG seeds do not establish a controlled causal comparison.

## MMG-010: synthesize without a rank decomposition seed

Use trace convention T=sum e_ij⊗e_jk⊗e_ki. T is invariant under cyclic rotation of the three tensor legs. GF(2) permits an exact quotient: store one coefficient per coordinate orbit and use rank-one term orbits of cost 1 (all factors equal) or 3 (otherwise). Canonical orbit signatures are XORs over the three rotations; since 3=1 in GF(2), a cyclic invariant tensor is represented faithfully. For n=2 the quotient has 24 coordinates, 15 singleton rank-one orbits and 1,120 triple orbits. Enumerate all singleton subsets of size ≤7 and all pairs of triple orbits; lookup matching residual in the singleton dictionary. 626,640 pair comparisons, 16,384 singleton subsets; no orbit decomposition with cost <7 and three rank-7 orbit decompositions found. Verify expanded witnesses against every formal tensor coefficient. This certifies the optimum within this cyclic dictionary; rank 7 for general 2×2 multiplication is known independently.

Embed the discovered ground in T_3 and complete remaining terms, then search replacement of up to three existing orbits with at most two dictionary orbits from a selected library of 51 coefficient vectors. 249 quotient coordinates, 44,251 enumerated orbits, 10,189,696 residual queries. Best remains rank 26; the known rank-23 constructions are better. No new rank bound.

## MMG-011: make addition cost part of the intention

For GF(2), every linear form in d inputs is a d-bit mask. A gate takes two available masks a,b and makes a XOR b available. For d=4, BFS of sets of available masks gives the minimum XOR circuit for any required set of output masks. The search has 1,857 reachable states (all start from the four independent input masks). Repeated forms are shared, and arbitrary cancellation is allowed. The linear operation model charges one field addition per XOR, copies are free.

For each rank decomposition synthesize three forward linear maps U,V,W^T. The last is transposed to decode. If all nodes are live and all inputs occur, reversing a linear circuit with d inputs, r outputs and s additions uses s+r-d additions: count its 2s+r edges and subtract d+s accumulator destinations. Hence total bilinear cost is r+s_U+s_V+s_W+r-d. Verify each forward and reverse circuit on all basis inputs; the actual interpreter counts the same reverse additions including multiple contributions to a signal. Pruning dead intermediate signals preserves the outputs and is required before using the transpose formula.

Matrix multiplication is invariant under (U,V,W)→(P U Q⁻¹,Q V R⁻¹,R W P⁻¹) in trace coordinates. Search the GL(2,2)^3 gauge orbit for each of two rank-7 seeds (standard and the cyclic synthesis), 432 candidates, verifying each full tensor. Best encoder counts 4,4,4 and decoder 7; total 15 additions, matching the classical Strassen-Winograd addition count. Exact linear synthesis conditional on each visited rank ground; not exhaustive over arbitrary rank-7 decompositions. For the earlier n=3 rank-26 witness, greedy common-subexpression extraction with randomized ties reduces additions 175→84; rank-preserving flip navigation with the addition objective reduces 84→46 (10+9+10+17 transpose correction). 1,200 attempted flips. Rank stays 26 and every retained improvement passes formal coefficient comparison. This is a better realization of our previous witness, not a new best-known 3×3 algorithm.

Tensor powers: for base leg dimension d=b², rank r, and s total additions for the base bilinear algorithm, the compiled exponentiation uses exactly r^k nonscalar products and s*(r^k-d^k)/(r-d) additions. Apply factored maps axiswise, expand neither full Kronecker matrix nor formal tensor. At b=3,k=4,n=81: 456,976 products, 1,218,770 additions (previously 4,636,625), 1,675,746 total field operations (previously 5,093,601). At b=2,k=6,n=64: 117,649 products, 567,765 additions (previously 681,318). Ten full comparisons with classical dense multiplication; every output coordinate verified. The exponent is unchanged: log_b(r). These are local serial executions, not E-CPU network runs. The operation counts exclude memory, host scheduling and copying, and the search/build cost is not counted as online execution.
