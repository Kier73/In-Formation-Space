# CLT-009 — Coupled sensors and transported action Grounds

The object of the experiment is an action, not merely a reconstructed observation. A source A owns a finite nonlinear action algebra over an eight-bit state. A separate B has a ten-bit physical state: the observable eight coordinates are concealed by a signed permutation, and two hidden coordinates evolve independently of the visible outputs (their own update depends on the visible state). B exposes two four-bit sensor channels. The learner gets nine explicitly aligned paired calibration observations (zero and the eight unit anchors), not the coordinate map or the source law of B. This is paired calibration, not autonomous alignment of unrelated streams.

The candidate chart is discovered by matching bit signatures and signs. Either channel alone leaves some target chart entries unresolved; both together determine the signed permutation within the declared grammar. Source A has three operations (rotate, nonlinear controlled XOR, toggle). A separate finite verifier exhaustively checks the candidate map for every B state and action against A, proving q(F_B(z,a))=F_A(q(z),a) on the entire declared domain. The verifier uses the actual source transition, not the discovered chart's prediction. An altered transition at a hidden calibration-excluded coordinate produces identical paired calibration but fails the full audit. This is not a sparse proof of arbitrary equivalence. No source authentication is asserted.

After qualification, reuse A's previously constructed action tables and powered word, rather than form B's own powered map. The target has no shared physical state identity with A and no target hidden-state reconstruction is claimed. All 256 observable anchors (247 outside calibration) are tested against independent raw B cycle indexing, for four concealed charts. Producer navigation does not perform new B transition calls after admission, but source formation, paired calibration, chart search and full B qualification all cost work. The held-out validation and realized final actions also invoke B. Report these separately; do not claim overall speedup, since the source oracle uses a cheap finite cycle baseline. No comprehensive CPU/memory meter.

Action sufficiency: after navigation the required intention is to achieve a demanded parity of observable bits 0 and 4, using HOLD or TOGGLE. Each original four-bit channel alone admits indistinguishable states that require opposite actions. Their joint representation is sufficient. A derived sensor, the parity itself, gives a two-class action-specific quotient and is sufficient for both demands. Computing that sensor is not free and its quotient does not generally close under the full transition alphabet. This is a terminal action Ground rather than a full continuation state. Validate all 512 local policy cases and each transported action against raw B. Different source law / missing observation / hidden-state controls retained.
